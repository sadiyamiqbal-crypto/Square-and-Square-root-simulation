Science of Fractional Distillation

Key concepts, working mechanism, and exam distinctions for Class 9 students.

1

Why does it work?

Crude oil is a complex mixture of hundreds of different hydrocarbons. Larger molecules have greater surface areas, resulting in much stronger intermolecular attractive forces. Consequently, large molecules demand significantly more heat energy to vaporize than smaller molecules.

2

The Fractionating Column

The tall fractionating column features a continuous temperature gradient: extremely hot (~370°C - 400°C) at the bottom, and cool (~25°C) at the top. As vapors ascend, each fraction condenses as soon as the temperature inside the column drops below its own specific boiling point.

3

Mixtures, Not Pure Compounds

Unlike water (100°C) or pure ethanol (78°C), fractions do not have sharp, single boiling points. Instead, each fraction (like kerosene or diesel) is a mixture of several hydrocarbons that boil within a narrow range of temperatures.

⚖️ Must-Know CBSE Comparison: Simple vs. Fractional Distillation

Feature Simple Distillation Fractional Distillation
Boiling Point Difference (ΔT) Requires a large difference (usually greater than 25 K / 25°C). Used when boiling points are very close (less than 25 K / 25°C apart).
Apparatus Required Distillation flask, thermometer, Liebig condenser, beaker. A special fractionating column packed with glass beads or bubble trays.
Nature of Process Single cycle of evaporation followed directly by condensation. Repeated, successive vaporization-condensation cycles along the column height.
Classic Textbook Examples Separating acetone (B.P. 56°C) and water (B.P. 100°C); salt from water. Crude petroleum refining; separation of different gases from liquid air.
